# Faceless Video Director — internal production skill

This is a checklist, not an automatic renderer and not permission to download internet content.

## Narrative first
1. Define audience, viewing promise, one key takeaway and target format.
2. Check the source and event date of each factual claim; never invent event footage or quotes.
3. Prepare and approve complete narration before dividing it into shots.
4. Measure or estimate real spoken timing; never cram narration into a short scene and call it finished.

## Shot design per scene
For each shot specify its role (hook, context, evidence, explanation, contrast, payoff or CTA), one concrete visual subject and action, a suitable medium (real image, footage, diagram, typography or 3D), camera direction, subtitle safe area and intentional transition.

Do not force all technology topics into circles, stars or networks. Template colours and effects are secondary to meaningful media. A user-provided image from the Video Studio Media Library should be matched to an approved scene and used with restrained deterministic pan/zoom. Never describe reconstructed or AI-generated imagery as a documentary photograph.

## Source and licensing
Only import local JPEG/PNG/WebP with an authentic source and explicit confirmation of commercial rights. Inspect people, trademarks and restrictions. Preserve creator, source and sha256 information. A checkbox is not proof of copyright clearance. Pexels licenses allow many commercial uses, but API access is not guaranteed. Do not scrape assets or infer permission from Google Image Search.

## Motion and pacing
Maintain one coherent brand identity for the film: palette, typography and camera language. Change scene layout when it improves storytelling; avoid clutter and back-to-back identical effects. Each camera move must reinforce a new fact or emotion, not merely imitate activity.

## Voice and QC gates
1. Real TTS WAV exists, is audible and is not longer than its approved slot. If TTS fails, mark the job failed, not narrated.
2. Listen to the final audio for correct words, clarity and intonation. RMS checks cannot prove speech accuracy.
3. Review opening, shot midpoints, transitions, closing, title safe areas and duplicated visuals. Do not equate successful encoding with visual quality.
4. Check caption synchrony against audible words, and music/SFX levels against narration.
5. Check source attribution, rights and platform synthetic-media/inauthentic-content policies before publishing.

Never guarantee monetization or automate mass-produced near-identical videos. If a quality gate cannot run, report it as not verified.

## Delivery artefacts
Approved script, storyboard, source notes, media provenance, final MP4, subtitle files, review report and unresolved creative warnings.
